§00 · The vision

One operator at a time,
we rebuild the room

The Nigerian creative economy is not going to be lifted by a policy paper. It is going to be lifted by a generation of operators who own their source — who write the story, run the tooling, keep the archive and publish from where they stand.

The Catalyst School exists to produce that generation. Not learners, not audiences — peers. We teach the doctrine (the Lagos Mafia Strategy), the operating system (Catalyst OS), and the posture (Structure · Clarity · Documentation · Calm). We hand you the artefacts, then we get out of the way.

A mafia that cannot recruit is just a small room. This is the recruiting.

§01 · Standing

Who this school is for

One person, one operator, more ambition than budget. Talented at the work. Trapped inside the model that pays for it.

You are already good — that is not in doubt. The problem is shape. You trade hours for money and stop earning when you stop moving. You do brilliant work for one client and rebuild the offer for the next. The frameworks live in your head, so nothing you have made can operate without you standing behind it.

The school is for the operator who has decided that will not do — and who is prepared to install a system in its place.

It is for you if

You are a Lagos creative, freelancer, agency-of-one, or a small founder. You can already deliver — the ceiling is structure, not skill. You want to own IP, not rent yourself out.

It is not for you if

You want a get-rich course, someone else's playbook applied to your life, or a room full of motivation. This is a workshop, not a stage.

§02 · The posture

Four pillars of the sovereign operator

The doctrine of the school in four lines. Anything the cohort builds is checked against these.

I

Structure beats enthusiasm

Immutable frameworks outlast temporary hype. When the grind begins, structure is the reason you do not collapse.

II

Clarity beats speed

Every movement is understood before it is executed. This is how you stop paying for the same mistake twice.

III

Documentation beats memory

If it is not written down, it does not exist. The archive is the studio's second brain and its only defence.

IV

Calm beats charisma

Surgical posture under pressure. Zero-explanation execution reads as planned transition, not panic.

§05 · Standard

What you leave with

The school is graded on artifacts, not attendance. If you finish, these exist.

  1. A productised offer with a fixed scope, a fixed price and a written delivery clock — priced so it stops trading your hours for money.
  2. At least one standing instrument — an agent, a script, a pipeline — that does a job you used to do by hand and now runs without you.
  3. A live publishing engine on your own land (Substack, site, repo) with the first six shipped pieces in the archive.
  4. A Master Control System workspace: one source of truth, a decision chain, a cash-clock protocol and a bad-day playbook you have rehearsed at least once.
  5. A Catalyst Stack map — every project you have ever run, ordered by layer, showing exactly what the next one inherits from it.
  6. A doctrine in your own voice — the version of the Lagos Mafia Strategy that describes how you now operate.

§ Bonus module · Ops

The Bad Day Playbook

Formlessness under pressure. Every scenario has a move already decided. Calm reads as planned transition, not panic — this is what the Sneakers Fest ops handbook taught the school.

Scenario 01

Power failure

Switch to backup immediately.

Public face: zero explanation, resume calmly. Nobody announces what nobody noticed.

Scenario 02

Rain / weather

Move critical rigs to sheltered ground.

Public face: redirect crowd via security; protect the kit, not the schedule.

Scenario 03

Vendor no-show

Reassign booth, shift signage.

Public face: no explanation given; keep the visual order intact.

Scenario 04

Crowd surge

Pause entry at 80% density.

Public face: security redirects traffic; a queue is safer than a crush.

Scenario 05

Government interference

Event lead engages privately; permits presented privately.

Public face: the crowd is shielded from it entirely.

Scenario 06

Cash lag on receivable

Paid beats promised. Nothing ships until the money is received.

Public face: hold the 15% doomsday reserve; never spend against a promise.

Drill protocol: three to five days before any live event, run the flight simulation until the response is instinctual.
